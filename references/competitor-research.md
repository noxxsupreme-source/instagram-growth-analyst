# Competitor research

Use this reference when the user requests public competitor comparison or content discovery.

## Select a fair comparison set

Prefer three to ten accounts that share the audience, topic, language, geography, or commercial goal. Include direct peers and a small number of aspirational accounts. Note material differences in account size, brand recognition, production budget, and publishing frequency.

Capture evidence with the account, post URL, public metric, and observation date. Public counts can change and do not reveal private reach, retention, or conversion.

## Compare patterns

Review enough recent posts to distinguish repeatable patterns from a single outlier. Compare:

- recurring topics and content pillars;
- opening frames, hooks, and promise types;
- duration, structure, pacing, and payoff timing;
- covers, captions, calls to action, and series formats;
- publishing cadence and observable audience response; and
- outlier posts relative to each competitor's typical public performance.

Normalize public engagement by an available denominator, but label it carefully. Views per follower is not reach rate, and engagement per view is not conversion.

## Turn research into original opportunities

Separate the result into:

- **Observed:** directly visible or measurable facts.
- **Inferred:** plausible strategic explanation.
- **Opportunity:** an original angle, unmet question, underserved audience, or format experiment.

Extract structures, not expression. Do not reproduce distinctive scripts, jokes, captions, visuals, music, branding, or shot sequences. Cite sources when browsing is used and clearly identify any inaccessible or uncertain data.
