# Creative audit

Inspect the complete asset when it is available. Do not infer unseen frames, audio, captions, or audience reactions.

## Diagnostic stages

- **Stop:** Does the cover or opening frame earn attention from the intended audience?
- **Hook:** In the first moments, is the subject, tension, or promised outcome clear?
- **Hold:** Does the pacing continually add information, emotion, proof, or visual change? Identify dead time and confusing transitions.
- **Reward:** Does the content deliver the promised value or entertainment at an appropriate time?
- **Share:** Is there a specific social or practical reason to send or save it?
- **Convert:** Does the post make the next action relevant and proportional to the viewer's intent?

For each meaningful issue, report:

1. the observable evidence, including a timestamp for video;
2. the likely viewer consequence;
3. the related metric, if available;
4. a specific revision; and
5. a simple A/B test.

Avoid generic prescriptions such as “make the hook stronger” or “post consistently.” Rewrite the exact hook or describe the exact edit. Preserve the creator's voice instead of forcing every post into the same high-energy style.

## Interpreting mismatches

- Strong stop but weak hold can indicate packaging that overpromises or a slow payoff.
- Strong retention but weak shares can indicate competent delivery without enough novelty, identity, utility, or emotion.
- High reach but low follows can indicate weak profile-content alignment, an unclear series promise, or an audience mismatch.
- Strong engagement from followers but weak non-follower reach can indicate insider context or packaging that assumes prior knowledge.

These are hypotheses. Phrase them as likely explanations and test them against comments, retention curves, and follow-up experiments.
